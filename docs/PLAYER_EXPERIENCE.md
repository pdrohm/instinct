# Player Experience

> The emotional journey. This document describes what the player should *feel*, told as moments — not the systems that produce them.
> If a system cannot trace back to a feeling described here, question the system, not this document.
> Companion to `GAME_DNA.md`. The systems that serve these feelings are governed by `DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md`.

## Why this document exists

Systems are easy to design and easy to over-build. Feelings are the point, and they are easy to lose. This document is the emotional target we tune *toward*. When a mechanic is "working" on paper but the moment feels flat, this document is the authority, not the spec.

Read these as short stories the player might live. None of them are scripted. All of them should be *possible* — and, if the game is working, common.

## The texture of the whole experience

Before the individual moments, the overall feel:

Most of the time, nothing dramatic is happening — and that is correct. The experience is mostly **quiet attention**: reading the world, weighing small risks, conserving energy, staying alive. Against that calm, the sharp moments — a chase, a near-death, a loss — land hard *because* they are rare. A game that is dramatic every minute has no drama at all. We are building a rhythm of long calm and short intensity, the rhythm of an actual life.

The player should end a session not with "I beat that" but with "I *lived* that."

## The moments

### Being hungry

It starts as a background unease and becomes the thing you cannot ignore. Hunger is not a number ticking down — it is pressure that slowly narrows your options and pushes you toward risks you would rather not take. The feeling we want: *the world was fine to just watch, until my own body started making the decision for me.*

### Watching a herd from a distance

Stillness, patience, calculation. You are not acting — you are *reading*. Which one is slower? Where is the wind? How far is too far? This is one of the most important feelings in the game: the sense that **observation is gameplay**, that doing nothing while you learn the situation is a real and rewarding choice.

### Realizing the chase may not be worth it

The quiet gut-check. You *could* run. But you count the cost — your stamina, your exposure, the distance — and you choose not to. The feeling we want: *restraint as mastery.* Walking away from a bad bet should feel as good as winning a good one. If it doesn't, the energy economy is mistuned.

### The commitment

The moment you decide *yes, now.* A small adrenaline spike — you have spent your caution and there is no taking it back. Whatever happens next is yours.

### Barely surviving

You made it, but it cost you. You are alive and worse off — exhausted, hungry, exposed. The feeling we want is not triumph but a shaky **exhale**: *that was too close, and I can't do that again for a while.* Survival should feel earned and slightly frightening, never clean.

### Escaping another predator

The flip. For a moment you stop being the one who acts and become the one who is *acted upon*. Fear, not strategy. The world was always bigger than you; here it reminds you. The feeling we want: *I was the hunter one minute and prey the next, and the world didn't announce the switch.*

### Returning to feed

Relief with weight. You made it back to safety or to food, and for a moment the pressure lifts. This is the game's inhale after the exhale — the calm that only means something because the danger was real.

### Watching migration

Awe at scale and indifference. The world moves on its own vast schedule, with or without you, and for a moment you are just a witness to something larger. The feeling we want: *quiet wonder* — the sense of being a small life inside an enormous living thing.

### Losing offspring / losing the herd

Genuine loss, not a fail state. Something you were bound to is gone, and the world does not pause to acknowledge it. This is the hardest and most valuable emotion the game can reach — the one that turns a survival sim into something people remember. It must be handled with care, never cheaply, and it is deliberately unresolved how far we push it (see `DESIGN_QUESTIONS.md`).

### Surviving winter / surviving scarcity

Endurance. A long stretch where the world offers less and asks more, and simply *continuing* is the achievement. The feeling we want: *I didn't win. I lasted.* Coming out the other side should feel like a real accomplishment precisely because it was grinding, uncertain, and never guaranteed.

### Finding safety

The rarest and most precious feeling: a place, a moment, a stretch of time where you can stop being afraid. Rest that you *earned* and that could be taken away. Safety only matters in a world where it is not the default — and it never should be.

## The emotional arc of a good session

A good session, whatever its length, tends to move through:

**Arrival** (getting your bearings, reading the world) → **pressure** (a need builds) → **decision** (weigh, commit or refrain) → **consequence** (the world answers honestly) → **aftermath** (better or worse, always changed) → **quiet** (a return to attention, carrying what just happened).

Not every session hits every beat, and that is fine. But if a session is *all* pressure and consequence with no quiet, or all quiet with no consequence, the rhythm is broken.

## What we are protecting against, emotionally

- **Flatness** — a session where nothing felt like it mattered.
- **Noise** — constant drama that leaves no room for the quiet that makes drama land.
- **Detachment** — the player thinking about systems and numbers instead of feeling like the animal.
- **Cheapness** — big emotions (loss, fear) triggered by unfair or arbitrary events rather than earned ones.
- **Comfort** — the creeping sense that the player is safe, in control, and central. That comfort is the death of every feeling on this list.

## The test

For any moment in the game, ask: **"Would a player tell someone else about this — and would they tell it as something that happened to *them*, not something the game did?"**

If yes, it belongs. If it is just a mechanic firing, it is not there yet.
